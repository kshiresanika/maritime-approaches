# Topic 02 — Guarding the Maritime Approaches

## Problem statement
The sea is now a grey-zone front: cut cables, shadow-fleet tankers, and weaponized sea
drones. You cannot patrol everything — so scarce assets have to be pointed at the right
vessel. Build a maritime situational-awareness tool for a port or approach that fuses
an electro-optical feed (coastal, patrol-vessel, or USV camera) with the live AIS
picture to flag dark vessels, AIS spoofing, and anomalous behaviour near critical
infrastructure — and produces evidence-grade output that tells an operator which
vessel, on what track, with what confidence, so a scarce patrol or boarding decision
can be aimed where it matters.

## Context
The maritime domain has become an active theatre of sabotage and provocation. Russia's
shadow fleet has been linked to more than a dozen damaged undersea cables in the Baltic
since late 2023, and the EU responded in February 2026 with a €347 million
cable-security package — its largest to date. Uncrewed surface vessels have graduated
from curiosities to weapons platforms.

The defender cannot watch every mile of coast and cable at once; patrol ships, aircraft
and boarding teams are scarce and slow. The real problem is deciding, out of thousands
of transits, which single vessel is worth sending a scarce asset after — and then
having enough of a case to act. Attribution is legally fragile: case after case has
failed to prove intent, and suspect vessels have been released.

## Operational scenario
A coastal sensor watches a busy approach near seabed cables. A tanker transits
broadcasting an AIS identity that does not match its observed class and heading, then
loiters over a cable route. The tool should flag the mismatch, fix its bearing and
track, and produce a concise, defensible record that justifies sending a patrol —
rather than drowning the operator in undifferentiated traffic.
